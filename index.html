    03 · Case studies

    How the systems paid off

    A product YouTube channel, from zero

    Problem
    An AI tool for Roblox creators needed signups without paid ads.
    Result
    Three videos past 100K views. Daily signups doubled and MRR grew 4.8× while I led growth and content.
    What I did
    Launched the channel and made every video myself. Tested comparisons, tutorials and Shorts. Read retention on every upload.
    What I cut
    Clipper campaigns pulled 1.2M views and almost no signups. I flagged it and pushed that effort into long-form, where weekly signups tracked views.
    ~260 to ~520daily signups while I led growth & content
    360K+views on my top 3 videos
    4.8×MRR over the same period

    Support that scales without more people

    Problem
    New users got stuck installing, connecting Roblox Studio and adding their first system, and the same questions landed in Discord every day.
    What I built
    One source of truth, two front doors. The Help Center gives a 3-step start path, copy-paste AI prompts and a full library catalog. The Discord helper reads the same pages, so a user who types "studio won't connect" gets the exact steps and screenshot, and only real problems become tickets.

    Dragon Bound: from an empty baseplate to a live game

    Problem
    Prove that one person can ship a real, public Roblox game, not a demo.
    How
    A build-test-ship loop: every change syncs into Roblox Studio, gets playtested with real measurements and screenshots, then goes live. Art is rendered in Blender and uploaded privately by script. Prices, XP and rewards all come from one economy formula, and telemetry tracks the onboarding, story and boss funnels.
    Play it on Roblox Watch the devlog
    48% to 0%creature animation stutter near the player, after one test pass
    ~50%less texture memory
    124live art assets moved to private
